published: written Toledo 1310; Berlin 1777 (published by "Jacob Shklower", per the Jewish Encyclopedia); ed. B. Goldberg and L. Rosenkranz, 2 vols., Berlin 1846–48 (*Liber Jesod Olam seu Fundamentum Mundi*, with an analysis by D. Cassel and a German translation of part 4)
language: Hebrew

why: The fullest medieval Hebrew handbook of astronomy and the calendar, written for Asher ben Jehiel: geometry and trigonometry, the spheres, eclipses, the length of the year, the Jewish calendar and the dates of history. The background to the calendar works of Bornstein and to *Ayil Meshulash*; the 1777 Berlin edition came from the Shklov circle (check whether it is linked to Barukh Schick, the Gaon's associate who translated Euclid).
scans: Berlin 1846–48: a library scan of vol. 1 is held by the owner (check the source library and look for vol. 2); Penn and Munich (BSB) hold copies; check the Frankfurt digital collections, Google Books and HebrewBooks. Berlin 1777: check HebrewBooks.
existing: No English translation found; part 4 is in German (Cassel, in the 1846–48 edition). Study: I. Wartenberg on its use of Euclid ([preprint](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/10105267/1/Wartenberg_15%20July%202020%20PRE-PRINT%20ILANA%20WARTENBERG.pdf)).
